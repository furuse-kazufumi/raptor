## Solution

**Setup:** Houses 1–4 (left→right), distinct colors {red, green, blue, yellow}, distinct pets {cat, dog, bird, fish}.

### Step 1 — Place the colors

- Clue (2): House 1 = **blue**. So red, green, yellow occupy houses 2, 3, 4.
- Clue (1): green is immediately right of red. Since house 1 is blue, red cannot be house 1, so the only adjacent red→green options are:
  - **Case A:** red=2, green=3 (then yellow=4)
  - **Case B:** red=3, green=4 (then yellow=2)

### Step 2 — Test each case against the pet clues

**Case A** (red=2, green=3, yellow=4):
- Clue (3): cat lives in the yellow house → cat in house 4.
- Clue (5): fish in house 4.
- Contradiction — two pets in house 4. **Case A is impossible.**

**Case B** (red=3, green=4, yellow=2, blue=1):
- Clue (3): cat in yellow house → cat in **house 2**.
- Clue (5): fish in **house 4**.
- Clue (4): dog is immediately left of the green house (house 4) → dog in **house 3**.
- Remaining pet (bird) goes to the only free house → bird in **house 1**.

Check all clues for Case B:
1. Green (4) immediately right of red (3). ✓
2. Blue is house 1. ✓
3. Cat in yellow house (house 2). ✓
4. Dog (house 3) immediately left of green (house 4). ✓
5. Fish in house 4. ✓

All five clues hold.

### Uniqueness

The color layout had only two possible arrangements (Case A and Case B); Case A was eliminated by a direct contradiction (clues 3 and 5 collide in house 4), leaving Case B. Within Case B the pets were **forced** with no free choices: cat by clue 3, fish by clue 5, dog by clue 4, and bird as the sole remainder. Therefore the arrangement is unique.
