## Key

Unique arrangement -> house 1: blue, bird; house 2: yellow, cat; house 3: red, dog; house 4: green, fish. Pet in the GREEN house = fish. A correct answer must give this arrangement (or at least the correct green-house pet) with uniqueness.

## Answer

**Solution**

Colors: Clue (2) fixes blue = house 1. Clue (1) requires red and green adjacent with green immediately right, so (red, green) ∈ {(2,3), (3,4)} — (1,2) is impossible since house 1 is blue.

- **Case A: red=2, green=3** → yellow=4. Clue (3) puts the cat in house 4, but clue (5) puts the fish in house 4. Two pets in one house — contradiction. Case A eliminated.
- **Case B: red=3, green=4** → yellow=2 (only slot left). No contradiction.

So colors are uniquely: 1=blue, 2=yellow, 3=red, 4=green.

Pets: Clue (3): cat → house 2 (yellow). Clue (5): fish → house 4. Clue (4): dog immediately left of green (house 4) → dog in house 3. Remaining bird → house 1. Every placement was forced, so the pet assignment is also unique.

| House | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Color | Blue | Yellow | Red | Green |
| Pet | Bird | Cat | Dog | Fish |

Verification: (1) green(4) is immediately right of red(3) ✓; (2) blue=1 ✓; (3) cat in yellow(2) ✓; (4) dog(3) immediately left of green(4) ✓; (5) fish in 4 ✓.

**Uniqueness proof:** Blue is forced to house 1 by (2). Only two red/green adjacencies exist given that, and Case A is refuted by the cat/fish collision in house 4, leaving exactly one color arrangement. Given the colors, clues (3), (5), (4) each force a single pet placement (cat→2, fish→4, dog→3), and the bird takes the only remaining house. No choice point remains, hence the arrangement is unique.

**The pet in the GREEN house (house 4) is the FISH.**
